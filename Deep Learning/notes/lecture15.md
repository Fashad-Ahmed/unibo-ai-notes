We have reached the pinnacle of modern AI: **Conditional Generation & Editing**.

Up to this point, our models have been brilliant but uncontrollable. If you sample random noise $z \sim N(0,1)$ and pass it to a GAN or a Diffusion model, it will generate a beautiful, photorealistic image—but you have no idea what it will be. It might be a dog, it might be a car.

To build tools like Midjourney, DALL-E, or Sora, we need **Control**. We need to mathematically force the generator to obey a prompt (the "condition" $c$).

Professor Asperti breaks this down into two distinct engineering paradigms: **Training-Time Conditioning** (baking the rules into the network) and **Sampling-Time Conditioning** (steering the network after it is built).

Here is the deep dive into the math and architecture of Conditional Generation.

---

### **1. Training-Time Conditioning (Explicit Conditioning)**

Instead of learning the probability of an image given noise $P(X|z)$, we change the fundamental mathematical objective. We must learn $P(X|z, c)$, where $c$ is the condition (e.g., a class label, or a CLIP text embedding of a prompt).

#### **The AC-GAN (Auxiliary Classifier GAN) Trick**

In early conditional GANs, researchers just fed the label $c$ into the Discriminator and asked, *"Is this a real dog or a fake dog?"*

The **AC-GAN** changed the game by forcing the Discriminator to do two jobs:

1. True/Fake detection.
2. **Auxiliary Classification:** Guessing the class label of the image.

**The Math & The Consequence:**
The loss function adds a new term: $-\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(x,c)}[\ln(q_\theta(c|x))]$. This forces the Generator to synthesize images that the Discriminator can *easily* classify.

* **The Benefit:** To make an image "easy to classify", the Generator pushes the image deep into the territory of that class, far away from ambiguous boundaries. This results in incredibly sharp, hyper-realistic images.
* **The Flaw (Bias):** Real life is ambiguous. By forcing images away from the boundaries, the AC-GAN learns a "biased" distribution. It generates caricatures of reality rather than true reality.

#### **Architectural Plumbing: How do we inject the text?**

You cannot just append a sentence to an image tensor. We have to inject the condition mathematically into the network's layers.

1. **Vectorization:** The brute-force method. Turn the label into a vector, duplicate it thousands of times, and stack it as a new "color channel" on the image.
2. **FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation):** Instead of stacking channels, we use the text prompt $c$ to generate two vectors: $\gamma$ and $\beta$. We multiply the network's feature maps by $\gamma$ and add $\beta$. The text is literally stretching and shifting the image features.
3. **AdaIN (Adaptive Instance Normalization):** Used in StyleGAN. We completely erase the mean and variance of the image layer, and replace them with the mean and variance generated by the condition.
4. **LoRA:** As we discussed earlier, we inject tiny $A \times B$ matrices into the Attention layers specifically to handle the condition without retraining the massive base model.

---

### **2. Sampling-Time Conditioning (Guidance)**

What if we don't want to retrain the model? What if we have a standard, unconditional generator, and we want to "hack" its latent space to steer it toward a specific output? This is called **Guidance**.

#### **A. Latent Space Exploration (GANs/VAEs)**

If you train a generator on human faces, the latent space (the universe of all possible $z$ vectors) organizes itself logically.

Researchers discovered that you can find specific **Vector Directions** corresponding to semantic concepts.

* Let's say you find the vector $\vec{v}_{glasses}$.
* If you have a latent point $z_{face}$ that generates a face without glasses, you can simply do vector addition: $z_{new} = z_{face} + \alpha \vec{v}_{glasses}$.
* When you decode $z_{new}$, the face will seamlessly grow a pair of glasses!

**The Entanglement Problem:**
Sometimes, attributes are mathematically tangled. If the dataset mostly contained older people wearing glasses, the vector $\vec{v}_{glasses}$ might accidentally contain the "old age" direction as well. Adding glasses would accidentally age the face. Engineers fix this by calculating orthogonal projections (e.g., forcing the glasses vector to be at a perfect 90-degree angle to the age vector).

#### **B. Classifier Guidance (Diffusion Models)**

In Diffusion models, we use a technique called **Guidance** to steer the thermodynamic denoising process.

At step $t$, we have a blob of static $x_t$. We want it to turn into a "sunflower".

1. We pass the static $x_t$ into an external, pre-trained Classifier.
2. The Classifier outputs a probability: *"How much does this static look like a sunflower?"*
3. We calculate the mathematical gradient of that probability: $\nabla_{x_t} \log p(\text{sunflower} | x_t)$.
4. We **physically add that gradient to the noise tensor** before taking the next diffusion step.

We are literally using the Classifier's gradient as a gravitational pull, dragging the static toward the "sunflower" manifold at every single step of the reverse diffusion process. (Modern systems like Stable Diffusion use **CLIP Guidance**, using a text-image alignment model instead of a simple classifier).

### **A Fascinating Theoretical Finding: The Universal Space**

Slide 32 mentions something mind-blowing about representation learning.
If you train a GAN to generate faces, and you train a VAE to generate faces, they use completely different math, different loss functions, and different architectures.

Yet, researchers found that **their latent spaces are nearly identical**.
You can define a simple linear map (a basic matrix multiplication) to translate a coordinate from the GAN's brain perfectly into the VAE's brain. This proves that deep learning models aren't just memorizing pixels—they are discovering the fundamental, universal mathematical geometry of the real world.
